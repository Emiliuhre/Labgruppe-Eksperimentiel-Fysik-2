\section{Determining the temperature of the Sun}
To determine the temperature of the sun we find the wavelength with maximum intensity, 
in each of the 3 measurements we took using equation \eqref{eq:WienDisplacement}.
We assume a constant $\pm 2 \text{ nm}$ uncertainty on the wavelength measurement,
which then becomes an uncertainty in the temperature measurement of $\sigma_T = |\frac{T_\text{max}}{\lambda_\text{max}}\sigma_\lambda|$, when we use Wien's displacement law.
The final estimate for the temperature is then given by a weighted mean of our 3 measurements, and becomes 
\[T = 5564.1 \text{ K} \pm 12.6 \text{ K}\]
\section{Determining salts and their concentrations}
If we plot the absorbtion spectra for the 3 solutions (figure \ref{fig:AbsorbtionSpectra}), we can compare them to the absorbtion spectra for the salts
$\text{CuSO}_4$,$\text{NiSO}_4$ og $\text{CoSO}_4$, and find out which solution is which.
We determined that the R solution contained $\text{CoSO}_4$, the G solution contained $\text{NiSO}_4$ and the B solution contained $\text{CuSO}_4$.
The relative number densities were also determined as 
\[N_{rel} = \frac{\text{max}(I_1)}{\text{max}(I)},\]
where $\text{max}(I_1)$ is the intensity at the most prominent absorbtion maxima of the first solution (i.e R1), and $\text{max}(I)$, is the intensity at the most prominent absorbtion maxima of the solution we are calculating the relative number density off.

\section{Precision of our Spectrometer}
The first challenge is calculating the wavelengths of the light from the outgoing angles that we measured.
From trigonometry we can see that $\theta_i = 60^\circ$, which means that we can calculate $n$ from equation \eqref{eq:Diffraction}.
We do this by numerically finding minima of the equation \eqref{eq:Diffraction}$ - \theta_o$, where $\theta_o$ here is the outgoing angle measured.
We then have the $n$ where the expected outgoing angle is closest to the measured one.
We can then calculate $\lambda$ from this $n$ by numerically solving for $\lambda$ in \eqref{eq:Sellmeier}.
If we then plot the wavelengths calculated in this way, together with their corresponding intensity, on top of the real spectra, we get figure \ref{fig:SpectrometerComparison}.
