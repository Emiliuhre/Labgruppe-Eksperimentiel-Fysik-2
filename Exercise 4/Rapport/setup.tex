\chapter{Experimental Setup}
\section{Measurement of Emission Spectra}
Measurements of emission spectra were done with a spectrometer made by OceanOptics \autocite{OcOptFlameManual2016}.
The spectrometer is connected to a fiber-optic cable, that is then directed towards the light source to be measured.
\subsection{Gas Lamps}
In the case of measuring the emission spectra of gas lamps\autocite{Frederiksen285570}, 
the cable was secured in a metal arm and pointed towards the gas lamp.
The position was chosen to be the point at which the measured intensity was the highest.
Measurements for a given element consists of firstly measuring the spectra for the gas, then turning the gas lamp off, 
and immediately measuring the background without moving the setup.
This was repeated for the elements, Argon, Hydrogen, Helium, Neon and Mercury.
\subsection{The Sun}
To measure the spectra of the sun, the spectrometer was carried outside and the cable was manually held such that it pointed towards the sun.
3 measurements were taken.
\section{Measurement of Absorbtion Spectra of Solutions}
To measure absorbtion spectra, a cuvette module\autocite{AbsorbtionModule} was inserted into the spectrometer. 
The measurements were simply to insert the cuvettes and take a measurement of the absorbtion spectra using the software that followed with the spectrometer.
Only a single measurement was made of each cuvette.

\section{Building a prism spectrometer}
The spectrometer was constructed according to the geometry illustrated in Figure~\ref{fig:PrismSpectrometerSetup}.
The setup uses a gas discharge lamp placed behind a narrow slit and a focussing lens, set at a distance equal to the focal length ($f=100\text{mm}$), in order to collimate the light. 
\begin{figure}
    \centering
    \includegraphics[width=0.45\textwidth]{../Figurer/NoBox.jpg}
    \caption{The setup with a white light LED shined through to disperse a complete rainbow as to recreate the Pink Floyd album cover of "The Dark Side of The Moon".}
    \label{photo:SetupNoBox}
\end{figure}
\looseness=-1
The collimated light is aimed at a 20 mm N-SF11 equilateral prism\autocite{ThorlabsPrism} at an angle of incidence $\theta_{\textup{i}} = 60^{\circ}$ as can be seen on the left side of Figure~\ref{photo:SetupNoBox}. 
The prism is on the central part of a turntable, but is kept at $\theta_{\textup{i}} = 60^{\circ}$. 
On a sweeping arm with same rotation axis as the turntable a detector\autocite{pasco_ci6604_manual} (gain=100) is placed with a focussing lens. 
Letting the detector orbit the prism in an appropriate range (visible with extra on both sides), 
we took time averaged measurements with PicoScope\autocite{PicoScope3203D} and noted the angle $\theta_o$.
A box with a hole was placed over the sensor and lens to mitigate background noise, 
since the signals from the emission lines are quite weak.

In order to not have the light beam hit the apex point of the prism it is pushed a little down. 
This offset of the prism means that the light beam has two interfaces.
To achieve precise placement, the prism was positioned such that its outermost and centralmost corners were 1.1 cm and 3.1 cm from the edge of the 6.1 cm diameter mounting circle, respectively. 
The detector assembly, consisting of a secondary focusing lens and a PASCO photo detector \autocite{pasco_ci6604_manual}, was mounted on a swinging arm with a pivot-to-sensor radius of 14.5 cm.
Notably, while the focusing lens also possessed a focal length of $f = 100$ mm, limitations of the equiptment meant that the lens had to be placed 60 mm before the detector. 
This is not optimal. 
The same gas discharge lamps as, when measuring with the OceanOptics spectrometer is used to be able to evaluate the succes of our spectrometer.
