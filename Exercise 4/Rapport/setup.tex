\chapter{Experimental Setup}
\section{Measurement of Emission Spectra}
Measurements of emission spectra were done with a spectrometer made by OceanOptics \autocite{OcOptFlameManual2016}.
The spectrometer is connected to a fiber-optic cable, that is then directed towards the light source to be measured.
\subsection{Gas Lamps}
In the case of measuring the emission spectra of gas lamps\autocite{Frederiksen285570}, 
the cable was secured in a metal arm and pointed towards the gas lamp.
The position was chosen to be the point at which the measured intensity was the highest.
Measurements for a given element consists of firstly measuring the spectra for the gas, then turning the gas lamp off, 
and immediately measuring the background without moving the setup.
This was repeated for the elements, Argon, Hydrogen, Helium, Neon and Mercury.
\subsection{The Sun}
To measure the spectra of the sun, the spectrometer was carried outside and the cable was manually held such that it pointed towards the sun.
3 measurements were taken.
\section{Measurement of Absorbtion Spectra of Solutions}
To measure absorbtion spectra, a cuvette module\autocite{AbsorbtionModule} was inserted into the spectrometer. 
The measurements were simply to insert the cuvettes and take a measurement of the absorbtion spectra using the software that followed with the spectrometer.
Only a single measurement was made of each cuvette.
\section{Building a prism spectrometer}
The setup of the spectrometer was much like seen in Figure 4, where the a gas discharge lamp was set in front of a small slit which was set in front of a collimating lens, as can be seen in on the right side of Figure 1.
\begin{figure}
    \includegraphics[width=0.50\textwidth]{../Figurer/NoBox.jpg}
    \caption{The setup with at white light shined through so a rainbow is visible as to recrate the Pink Floyd album cover of "The Dark Side of The Moon".}
\end{figure}
This arrangement was sat on top of a turntable so the light passing through the collimating lens hits a prism at a an angle of incidence $\theta_{\textup{i}} = 60^{\circ}$ as can be seen on the left side of Figure 1. 
The prism is on turning part of the turntable so the mounted sensor at an angle of $\theta_{\textup{o}} = 0^{\circ}$ is pointed orthogonal to the side of the prism opposite to the side that the beam from the collimating lens hits.
An other lens was placed in front of the sensor to focus the lines to the sensor. 
A box with a hole was placed over the sensor and lens to mitigate background noise, since the signals from the emission lines are quite weak.
The emission lines of ionized helium as a test can be seen in Figure 3.
\begin{figure}
    \includegraphics[width=0.50\textwidth]{../Figurer/Lines.jpg}
    \caption{The visible emission lines of helium shown through the lens in front of sensor.}
\end{figure} 
The sonsor was set at a gain of 10.
Diameter of swiveling mounting circle 6,1 cm.
Prism placement: The outermost corner of the prism is 1,1 cm from circle edge.
The centralmost corner of the prism is placed 3,1 cm from the same circle edge.
First (collimating) lens placement is the focal length $f=100\text{mm}$ from the gas lamp.
From the detector arm paning center to the detector is 14,5 cm.
The second lens (focusing) is 6 cm before the detector despite the focal length being 100 mm.
The same gas discharge lamps as, when measuring with the OceanOptics spectrometer is used to be able to evaluate the succes of our spectrometer.
